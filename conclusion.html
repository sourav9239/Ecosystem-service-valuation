Conclusion & Recommendation

This study describes the significant relation between LULC changes and impact on the function and structure of the ecosystem services. The amount of service value lost as a result of LULC transformation must be examined on a temporal and spatial level. The study provides insights into the ecological contributions of urban landscapes by combining valuation frameworks, land use/land cover (LULC) classification, and geospatial analysis.

Vital ES have been damaged as a result of the fast urbanization that has turned natural landscapes into populated regions. Services were impacted as a result of the loss of water bodies and green space. The importance of ecosystem services in improving urban resilience and livability is highlighted by this study. Maintaining a balance between growth and ecological preservation becomes increasingly important as metropolitan areas continue to expand. Therefore, in order to realize the benefit of all land uses and ecosystem services, appropriate ecological protection must be adopted. To support ES provision, we advise promoting the growth and management of green areas, urban forests, and wetlands. We also suggest putting forward methods for frequently evaluating ecosystem health and service delivery to guide adaptive management plans.
